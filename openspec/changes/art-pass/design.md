# Design

## Context

See proposal.md for why. Today (main at 0a4d447, after `crossing-shark-sync`, `coast-cay` and `status-sync`) every world visual is `_draw` code: `DevIslandTerrain.draw` (one rect per cell from a 7-entry `COLORS`, magenta for the other 8 terrains, used by `SandbarIsland`, `DevIsland` and `MovementPlayground`), `Palm._draw`, `Fire._draw`, `Workbench._draw`, `RainCatcher._draw`, `BuildSite._draw`, `Raft._draw` (18×10), `WorldItem._draw` (one tan 6×6 square), `Creature._draw` (a 5 px circle and a facing line, coloured by domain), `IslandSign._draw` (a 6×8 outline), `DrinkSpot._draw` (an 8×8 outline) and `Crossing._draw` (a blue rect with scrolling lines and a brown 64×32 deck). The player is one `Sprite2D` named `Sprite` with the 16×16 `player_placeholder.png`, a 6 px `CircleShape2D` and a `MultiplayerSynchronizer` replicating 20 properties (position, energy, model state, movement, stealth, downed, inventory, the meters and, since `status-sync`, `status_flags`). There is no TileMap, no Y-sort and no shadow anywhere. The world lives in `GameShell`'s `WorldView/Viewport/World`: the island (`SandbarIsland`, which now builds any layout: the 80×72 sandbar and the 88×64 coast cay, with a `Palms` child) and the `CoopSession` (`Entities`, `Pickups`, `Players`, each filled by a `MultiplayerSpawner`, plus the workbench, routes, raft site, signs, drink spots and crossings added in code). Day and night are a `CanvasModulate`; the *Suave* pixel filter is a Scale2x shader on the world viewport.

There are 14 items (the sandbar's ten and the coast cay's `nails`, `mirror`, `aloe`, `lighter`) and 3 creatures. The crossing's reef shark is spawned through the session's entity spawner (`CoopSession.spawn_creature`) with a host-only `lifetime_s`; `Creature.advance` returns before `brain.tick` while it has a lifetime, so its `state_id` stays 0 (`PATROL`) and its `facing` stays `DOWN` on every peer. A `Crossing` node sits at (0, 0) in the session while its sea, deck and riders are near `origin` (about y −4096). A raft whose crossing ends intact is beached at the raft site with the integrity it has left (`SandbarIsland.beach_raft`).

Data hooks already exist and are all empty: `ItemData.sprite` ("16x16 icon"), `CreatureData.sprite_frames`, `NpcData.sprite_frames` ("32x32 world sprite"), `BackgroundData.sprite_frames` ("32×32 frames"), `RecipeData.icon`, `PoiData.map_icon`, portraits.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- One look across terrain, sea, players, creatures, items, structures and the crossing, all on one master palette with one outline rule.
- Art as data: a new item or creature is a `.tres` plus its art, never a script edit.
- Exact same picture logic on every peer from state they already have; drawing never feeds back into the simulation.
- Cheap to extend: hand-made pieces are rough first and refinable, so every rule is checked by a test rather than by taste.

**Non-Goals:**
- Anything in the proposal's "Later" list; no new gameplay state, no networking of state the host keeps today (palm counts, fire burn-out, rain cups), no change to collision, movement, AI or crossing rules, no interpolation of creature positions.
- Runtime paper doll layers, skin or hair choice, background looks.

## Decisions

**D1. Character frames and anchor.** Players use 32×32 frames with the MetroCity figure. Every frame is placed with one fixed **feet line**, the bottom edge of the standing frame's lowest opaque row (row 30), 4 px below the body's position, centred on its x: the frame's own bottom edge is 5 px below the position and, with a 29 px figure, the head top sits about 25 px above it, about one tile into the row above (SPD's layout scaled up). Because the line is fixed, not measured per frame, the cycle keeps its 1 px bob and its 2–3 px side lift. The rim step (D8) recolours MetroCity's own edge ring, so the figure stays 14×29 with its feet on row 30 and these numbers, the water line (D19) and the head anchors (D20) hold after rimming. The 6 px collision circle, the body position and every movement rule stay as they are. The `Sprite2D` named `Sprite` is replaced by a `Look` node holding an `AnimatedSprite2D` named `Sprite` (path `Look/Sprite`); nothing outside the look reads it, and `test_player_scene.gd` moves to the new path (task 5.7). *Alternatives:* 16×24 frames (SPD's ratio) would need every human redrawn; 32×32 footprints (DCSS) cover four cells and misread pick-up and pathing; a per-frame "lowest opaque row" anchor would flatten the bob.

**D2. Facing from drawn motion, on every peer.** Every peer, the host included and the owner of a downed body included, derives a player's facing from the change of the body's drawn position over the last 0.1 s: a dead zone of 0.05 tiles/s keeps the last facing; the larger axis wins and a tie faces sideways. Velocity is not used: on the host a client's body advances 0 to `MAX_STEPS_PER_FRAME` queued intents a frame, so its velocity goes stale while intents stall, and a downed owner stops predicting while its body still moves by interpolation when dragged. Nothing is added to the replicated properties. Cost: teammates turn 100 ms late, as they already move 100 ms late, and a floater drifting on a current turns to face the drift. *Alternative:* a replicated `net_facing` would widen this change into coop-networking for a cosmetic.

**D3. Pose table and frame budget.** One `SpriteFrames` per character with these animations (3 facings each unless noted; side frames face right and are mirrored):

| State (replicated) | Animation | Unique frames | fps |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| standing, walk or run, not moving | `idle_*` | MetroCity frame 0 (shared with walk) | – |
| `WALK` moving | `walk_*` | 6 (MetroCity cycle) | 8 |
| `RUN` | `walk_*` | same 6 | 12 |
| `CROUCH` | `crouch_*` | 2, hand-edited from frames 0 and 1 (0 and 3 are identical in the down and up facings): head and torso 6 px lower, knees bent | 4 moving, frame 0 still |
| `CRAWL` | `crouch_*` | same 2 | 2 |
| `SWIM` | `swim_*` | 2, hand-edited arm strokes from frames 1 and 4 | 4 |
| `FLOAT` | `swim_*` | frame 0 | – |
| `DIVE` or stealth `SUBMERGED` | `dive` (fx) | 2-frame ripple ring, body hidden | 4 |
| downed | `downed` (side only) | 2, lying; the second lifts the chest 1 px | 1 |
| `DRAGGED` | `downed` | same 2, facing the drag | 1 |
| dead | `dead` (side only) | 1, the downed frame with eyes shut | – |

That is 33 unique frames (budget 48). Run reuses walk frames at a higher fps instead of new art (SPD's frame holds). A tied rider and the remaining poses are Later.

**D4. Baked base sheet with key ramps.** `tools/art/compose.py` stacks one MetroCity skin row, one outfit and one hair style into `characters/balsero_base.png`, drops the left facing (mirror) and lays the frames out per animation; the hand-made poses are then drawn on the same sheet. The clothing is drawn in a 4-shade **clothing key ramp**; skin (3 shades) and hair (3 shades) use their own **reserved key ramps** that this pass leaves unswapped, so a later change can offer skin and hair colours with data only. *Alternative:* a runtime paper doll (DCSS `tiledoll.cc`) costs three synced `AnimatedSprite2D`s per player for variety this pass does not offer; it returns with hair styles and backgrounds.

**D5. Look slots in the spawn payload.** `CoopSession._add_player` picks the lowest slot in 0–3 that no body in `_bodies` holds and spawns `{"peer", "position", "look"}`; `_spawn_player` reads `look` on every peer (an int in 0–3, else 0) into the body's plain `look_slot` field. `_bodies` can hold five bodies: the cap counts `_bodies.size() - _dropped.size()`, so with four players one of whom dropped, a new identity spawns a fifth body while the dropped one waits out its 90 s. Then no slot is free and the new body takes the lowest slot held by a dropped body; the two share a colour until the dropped body is removed. If that dropped player reclaims within the window (possible only once another connection frees), they keep their slot and the share lasts until one of them leaves: rare and cosmetic, accepted rather than re-sending a slot after spawn. The `MultiplayerSpawner` re-sends spawn data to late joiners, and a rejoin reclaims the same node (`coop-reconnect`), so both keep the slot with no new replicated property. Removing a body frees its slot. Solo is slot 0.

**D6. One player shader.** A `CanvasItem` has one material, so the palette swap and the water line (D19) are one shader, `player_look.gdshader`, with a `water_line` uniform the look turns on while swimming or floating; the shark's `waterline.gdshader` and the sea shaders share `art_common.gdshaderinc` (key-ramp lookup, water-line cut, the modulate varying). The swap replaces each texel that exactly equals a key-ramp colour with the same shade from the slot's row of `palette/player_ramps.png`. In a `canvas_item` `fragment()`, `COLOR` on entry is already the texture sample times the vertex colour, so multiplying the mapped texel by it would darken the clothing by the key colour; instead `vertex()` copies the vertex `COLOR` (the node's modulate) into a varying and `fragment()` multiplies the mapped texel by that. The varying carries modulate whichever way the renderer applies the day-night `CanvasModulate`; the 1-player night check confirms that tint also survives. Slot ramps (DawnBringer colours): 0 azul (mid `#5b6ee1`), 1 rojo (`#ac3232`), 2 amarillo (`#fbf236`), 3 verde (`#6abe30`); their mid tones' smallest pairwise contrast ratio is rojo–azul at about 1.5:1, above the 1.3:1 rule, and red against green differs by about 2.8:1 in brightness. *Alternatives:* four baked sheets (4× upkeep); a flat `modulate` tint (Brogue's per-cell tint flattens shading); two materials (a `CanvasItem` cannot hold both).

**D7. Master palette.** DawnBringer's 32-colour palette (DB32) plus at most 32 added colours: turquoise shallows and open-sea ramps, sand and dune ramps, foam, palm greens, one MetroCity skin ramp and the key ramps. DB32 is the larger sibling of the DB16 palette DawnLike is drawn on, by the same author, and DawnLike's credit already names DawnBringer; but no DawnLike colour is in DB32 (measured), so DawnLike is remapped like every other pack rather than spending 16 of the 32 added colours on its DB16 set. Stored as `palette/master_palette.png` (one pixel per colour) with a `.gpl` for editors and a named **outline set** (the darkest 4 entries) in art tuning. Kyrise (111 colours), Zelda-like (136), Scallywag (93), DawnLike (16) and MetroCity pieces are remapped to it and fixed by hand where the remap bands; the remap also strips baked half-transparent shadows (Zelda-like props, logs and rocks). Partial alpha is allowed only in `shadows/` and `decals/`, using the darkest colour. The three UI 9-slices in `art/ui/` (Kenney Pixel UI, used by the ui-shell theme) keep their colours until a UI pass, and `art/characters/player_placeholder.png` is exempt until the player sheet replaces it.

**D8. Pipeline tools.** `tools/art/` holds standard-library Python run with `python -I`, reading the git-ignored packs as data and writing only into `game/assets/art/`: a PNG reader and writer, `remap.py` (nearest master colour, reports what changed, drops half-transparent pixels outside shadows and decals), `rim.py` (DCSS `add_rim` adapted: a piece with its own edge ring, such as MetroCity's skin-and-grey edge or Kyrise's black outline, has those edge pixels recoloured to the nearest outline colour, so it keeps its size; only a piece with no edge ring gets a 1 px ring grown round its silhouette, per cell), `compose.py` (D4), and `check.py` (the palette, alpha and outline rules, for quick local runs). Every tool takes `--packs <dir>` (default `game/assets`), because the packs are git-ignored and not shared between worktrees: a worktree build points it at the main checkout's `game/assets`, or runs `tools/fetch_assets.py` there first. The GUT tests are the gate, because CI has no packs: they read only tracked files.

**D9. Tracked folders and licences.** `game/assets/art/{palette,terrain,decals,sea,characters,creatures,items,structures,shadows,fx}/`. Each folder's `License.txt` has one block per source, each with `Licence:` and `Source:` lines: a pack block also has the pack, author, version and the files derived from it, and its source is the pack's page, never a licence deed; an own-work block's source is "90 Millas (own work)" and its licence line reads "All rights reserved (see LICENSE)": the owner chose on 2026-11-08 to keep the project's own code and art all rights reserved for now, and the repository's `LICENSE` says so. The two Kenney licence files tracked today (`art/ui/`, `art/characters/`) name only kenney.nl and the CC0 deed, so task 1.3 appends a source block with the pack page to each. The old `art/characters/player_placeholder.png` is deleted with its import. Every tracked PNG imports with lossless compression and no mipmaps (checked by reading each `.import`).

**D10. Credits data and screen.** `game/assets/art/credits.tres` holds `ArtCredit` entries (pack, author, licence, licence address, source, version, modified, attribution required, Spanish line), class in `game/scripts/art/`. The new `credits_screen.tscn` in `game/scenes/shell/` lists the CC-BY entries first ("Con licencia CC BY 4.0"), then the CC0 packs ("Dominio público (CC0); gracias a…") and the OFL fonts, in a `ScrollContainer`, with *Volver*. CC BY 4.0 §3(a)(1) asks for the licence's address and, where practicable, the source's; `CREDITS.md` and `License.txt` are not resources and do not ship in an exported build, so each CC-BY line on the screen shows, as plain text, the source page and `https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/`. `TitleScreen` gains *Créditos* before *Salir*. One test checks that the `.tres`, `CREDITS.md` and every CC-BY `License.txt` agree. Wording: "DawnLike – 16x16 Universal Rogue-like tileset v1.81, de DragonDePlatino; paleta de DawnBringer (CC BY 4.0) – opengameart.org/content/dawnlike-16x16-universal-rogue-like-tileset-v181", "Tiny, Tiny Heroes - Animals, de Kacper Woźniak (thkaspar) (CC BY 4.0) – thkaspar.itch.io/tth-animals", "Kyrise's Free 16x16 RPG Icon Pack © 2018 Kyrise (CC BY 4.0) – kyrise.itch.io/kyrises-free-16x16-rpg-icon-pack", each marked *modificado* where recoloured, under a line with the licence's address.

**D11. Terrain as tile layers built from the grid.** `TerrainArt` (pure, static, in `game/scripts/art/`) maps a `GridTerrain` cell to (source, atlas coordinates, alternative, variant) using `terrain/terrain_art.tres`: per terrain kind its layer, its 16-tile corner set (corner mask → atlas coordinates) or its plain tile, and its variants. The island builds, every peer the same way and for any layout (sandbar, coast cay), these `TileMapLayer`s under itself: `Sand`, `Dune` and `Brush` (corner sets), `Plain` (one tile each for ROCK, SHINGLE, CLIFF, HAMMOCK, CASUARINA, REEF_FLAT, CORAL, MANGROVE and WRECK_INTERIOR until their own sets come), `Shore` (foam, D13) and `Decals` (tide pools and shade). Layer membership is cumulative, so each layer lies whole on the one below: `Sand` counts every land kind as land, `Dune` counts DUNE and SCRUB, `Brush` counts SCRUB (on the sandbar, dune-only membership would leave 60 lone dune cells and 68 seams; cumulative leaves 1 and 1). Cells are set with `set_cell`, not Godot's terrain painting, whose random alternatives and blob matching are not ours to reproduce. A corner is land when all four cells that share it are land. Zelda-like gives 13 of the 16 corner tiles per set (its 2×2 island block and 3×3 pond block, plus the full tile); the islet (no land corner; the pond centre is not one) and the two diagonal tiles are hand-made for each of the sand, dune and brush sets, 9 tiles in all. The edge tiles come with their own opaque water strip and white foam rim: both are keyed out so the water side is transparent over the sea and the only foam is D13's. Every terrain, shore and decal `TileSet` has zero physics, navigation and occlusion layers: the player's body collides on mask 1 with `move_and_collide`, and a `TileMapLayer` collides by default once its tile set has a physics layer, so one added while authoring would make art block movement. `DevIslandTerrain.draw` is replaced by the same builder in `DevIsland` and `MovementPlayground`; `GridTerrain`, `TerrainQuery` and `CreatureNav` are untouched.

**D12. One sea shader, two uses.** `sea/world_sea.gdshader` on one rect under the island: a seamless 32×32 water texture (recoloured from Scallywag's deep-water frames) drifting at 5 px/s (SPD's water speed), and a depth mask texture with one texel per cell (deep, shallows, brackish) that picks the palette step, so shallows and brackish water are one step lighter. The crossing uses the same shader with an all-open-sea mask and its own `_scroll_px` as the vertical offset. Both take modulate through the varying of D6. The island's sea runs on the shader clock and keeps moving under solo pause, which is cosmetic.

**D13. Shoreline on the land side, one foam line.** Both sources are mid-tile shorelines: a Zelda-like edge tile has sand to about row 10 then its own foam and water, and Eldiran's straight frame has land to row 6, foam to row 11, water below. Placing Eldiran's frames on the water cell, with the land keyed out, would float the foam 4–8 px off the beach, and keeping the Zelda-like rim would draw a second foam line. So, as DCSS `tilecell.cc` does (its `TILE_WAVE_*` overlays go on the floor cell beside water, not on the water cell), the foam goes on the **shore cells**: land cells whose `Sand` tile has at least one water corner. Each gets the foam piece for the same corner mask on the `Shore` layer, above the land layers: a straight edge (Eldiran's straight frames, shifted toward the land so the foam starts on the row where the sand tile's sand ends, rotated with alternative tiles), a concave and a convex corner (Eldiran's 16×33 curve cut at its empty row into two 16×16 tiles, hand-trimmed), and the diagonal and islet pieces composed from them by hand; brown, magenta and the label black are keyed out. Water cells get no foam. The 8 frames are played by a small shader on the `Shore` layer; each cell's phase is computed in GDScript as `hash(layout id, cell) % 8` and baked into a phase mask texture with one texel per cell (as D12's depth mask), which the shader samples at the fragment's cell (its world position from `vertex()`). GLSL has no GDScript `hash()` and a `TileMapLayer` gives a shader no per-cell data, and Godot's tile animation "random start times" is not under our control and need not match across peers or rebuilds.

**D14. Seeded variants.** Variants and offsets come from `hash(layout id, cell)` (no run seed reaches the island yet; creatures-ai's spawner uses its fixed seed 90). No art code calls a `RandomNumberGenerator` the simulation owns.

**D15. Y-sort without reparenting.** `y_sort_enabled` on `World` (game_shell.tscn), the island root and its `Palms`, the `CoopSession` root and its `Entities`, `Pickups` and `Players`, and the same nodes in the dev scenes. No node moves, so `MultiplayerSpawner.spawn_path`s and RPC paths are unchanged. Ground layers sit at negative `z_index` and never sort with actors at 0: sea −40, sand −30, dune −29, brush −28, plain −27, shore −26 (foam over every land edge, a plain rock at the coast included), decals −10. The crossing's sea rect is at −40 and its deck sprite at −20: the `Crossing` node sorts at its own (0, 0) while its riders and the passing shark are about 4,000 px north, so without those z values a Y-sorted session would draw the crossing's sea over them. Each actor's origin is its foot point: player body position, creature position, pick-up position, palm trunk base, the lean-to's back edge (D18), structure base, sign foot. Shadows are child sprites at relative `z_index` −1, under every actor. The name tag and speech bubble draw at `z_index` 20 so a crown never hides them.

**D16. Creatures.** `Creature` gets a `Look` (`AnimatedSprite2D` from `data.sprite_frames`) and a `Shadow`; `_draw` goes. `CreatureAnim.pick(names, state, facing, moving)` (pure) tries `<state>_<facing>`, `<state>`, `move_<facing>`, `move` (only while moving), `idle_<facing>`, `idle`. `moving` is "the drawn position changed within the last 0.15 s", tracked by the look on each peer: a client receives creature positions every 0.1 s (`SYNC_INTERVAL_S`, `REPLICATION_MODE_ALWAYS`) and never interpolates them, so a per-frame test would flip between `move` and `idle` five frames in six. Per-creature drawing data lives in `creatures/creature_art.tres` (`CreatureArt`, keyed by creature id, art data, not content): shadow size, the **airborne states** (lifted 8 px, shadow left on the ground, `move` frames whatever the motion; SPD `CrystalWispSprite`), the **perch state** with its lift, and whether it is drawn at the water line. Shark (DawnLike shark and fin, kitbashed, 32×16): `patrol` and `circle` (the same 2-frame fin glide), `strike` (3 frames: fin rises, splash), `idle`, drawn with the water-line shader so only the fin is opaque over a dark body shape; no shadow. The crossing's passing shark never ticks its brain, so it keeps state 0 and facing `DOWN` on every peer and plays `patrol`, the same glide; no payload or field is added for it. Crab (hand-made 16×16 side-on, from DawnLike colours with Kenney Tiny Dungeon `tile_0110` as a shape reference): `idle`, `move` (2-frame scuttle), `steal` and `pinch` (2-frame raised claws). Gull (TTH): `idle_side/down/up` (4 standing frames) and `move_side/down/up` (4 flying frames); airborne in `SWOOP`, `FLEE` and `RETURN`. In `PERCH` it sits at its nest, which the spawner sets to the nest palm's cell centre, the palm's trunk base: the perch lift (about 34 px) raises its sprite to the crown's nest point (D18), it has no shadow of its own, and its look sorts 1 px south of the trunk base (its node 1 px lower, its sprite 1 px higher) so it draws over the crown and behind anyone south of the palm. The state comes from the synced `state_id`, so host and client agree.

**D19. **D20. Head anchor.** Each animation has a head anchor offset (standing −26, crouch −20, swim −14, dive −4, downed −10 px from the body position) in `characters/balsero_art.tres` (`BodyArt`: frames, feet offset, water line, head anchors). `NameTag` (today a fixed −22), `SpeechBubble` (today a fixed `OFFSET` of (0, −14), "above a 16×16 character", which would put its label on a 29 px figure's face) and inventory-hud's `BodyMarks` read it. inventory-hud's interact prompt over a player target sits above the head anchor instead of its fixed 34 px; over other targets (pick-ups, structures) its 12 px offset above the origin stays, which this change records rather than changes.

**D21. Readable-art checks.** In `art_tuning.tres` (`ArtTuning`, `game/scripts/art/`; at `game/resources/art_tuning.tres` beside the other tuning files), which also holds the `master_palette.png` and `player_ramps.png` textures, so the palette files are referred to like every other tracked image: `contrast_min` 3.0 (WCAG ratio), `slot_contrast_min` 1.3, `max_unique_frames` 6, `max_creature_frames` 4, `max_character_frames` 48, `max_fps` 12, the outline set and each ground's mean colour. Contrast: on land the closed outline carries it, since every outline colour reaches 4.9:1 or more against sand (`#d9a066`), light sand and brush (`#6abe30`), so the check is that every outline colour reaches `contrast_min` against each land ground (a palette check); a per-frame fill rule was rejected because ordinary fills fail it (an orange crab 1.4:1, azul 1.9:1, skin 1.4:1 on sand). On water the outline colours fall to 1.7–3.1:1 against deep water (`#306082`), so swimmers (with the ripple) and the fin must reach `contrast_min` with the better of their outline ring and their mean colour against shallows and deep. Frame budgets apply to actor and structure `SpriteFrames`; terrain, sea and foam overlays are exempt (the shoreline has 8 distinct frames). Alpha: partial alpha only under `shadows/` and `decals/`. Items also: icon 16×16, item inside the 14×14 box one pixel in, no two identical alpha masks.

**D22. Drawing never writes.** Art nodes join the `art_view` group and only read: body states, creature `facing` and `state_id`, item ids, structure fields, crossing integrity and scroll. A test runs a scripted solo sandbar twice, with art and after freeing every `art_view` node, and compares per-frame snapshots; it also checks that the player's synchronizer lists the same property paths both times, without pinning their number, which coop-networking and survival-meters own (`status-sync` already made it 20).

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [MetroCity people (29 px) are taller than the Zelda-like hero (20 px) its doors and houses were drawn for] → this pass takes only terrain, logs, small props and the sign board from Zelda-like; the lean-to and every building are drawn at our scale.
- [Scallywag is drawn straight down, the rest in 3/4 view] → only its water texture, foam ideas, logs and frond clumps are used, which read the same from any angle.
- [Remapping 16–136-colour packs to one palette bands or muddies them; DawnLike's DB16 colours are 8–39 off their nearest DB32 entries] → rough first, hand fixes, and the checks enforce membership, not beauty.
- [A custom shader that writes `COLOR` drops modulate, and one that multiplies by the fragment's `COLOR` darkens by the texel twice] → every art shader multiplies by the vertex colour carried in a varying (D6); a 1-player night check in tasks.
- [Facing from motion lags teammates by 100 ms and turns floaters with the current] → accepted (D2).
- [Creature positions reach clients at 10 Hz without interpolation, so creatures step on clients] → existing behaviour; the art keeps one animation through the steps (D16) and does not add interpolation.
- [Palm coconuts, fire burn-out and rain cups are host state that clients do not receive today] → art draws what each peer holds: exact for the host and solo, possibly stale on a client until the queued "palm counts synced to clients" follow-up; no networking in this change.
- [The art coverage rule makes every new item and creature carry art, and the silhouette rule means each item needs its own icon, not a shared placeholder] → a rough 16×16 icon is minutes of work; this change draws the coast cay's four, `secret-cay`'s four items and later batches add theirs; the test names what is missing.
- [`ui-shell` and `inventory-hud` are active changes with requirements this one contradicts (the title's button list, the 10/16 crouch squash)] → task 0.2 adds MODIFIED deltas for whichever is archived before apply; otherwise whichever archives second reconciles (Workflow follow-up).
- [The look slots rely on `coop-reconnect`'s reclaiming, which is merged but not archived] → it archives before this change (D24).
- [Y-sort across the island and the session needs every node in between sorted] → a test walks a player across a palm's foot line and checks the draw order flips; z values keep the crossing's sea under its riders.
- [`test_title_screen.gd` (ui-shell) counts the title buttons; `test_player_scene.gd` pins the 16×16 placeholder and the `Sprite` path] → both are updated in the tasks that change them (2.1, 5.7).
- [CC-BY 4.0 §2(a)(5)(B) forbids DRM that restricts the licensed files] → the raw files stay in the public repo; recorded for any store build.
- [Downloaded archives are untrusted] → the tools only parse PNGs and copy bytes, keep the zip-slip guard, and call 7-Zip only when present.
- [The packs are git-ignored and absent from worktrees] → `--packs` on every art tool (D8).
- [MetroCity is "in development"] → `License.txt` and `CREDITS.md` record versions 1.0 and 2.0 and the read date.
- [6 tile layers on the 80×72 sandbar or the 88×64 coast cay] → about 35,000 `set_cell` calls once per island build, on every peer; one island is live at a time; measured in the smoke run.

## Migration Plan

- Order: pipeline and checks first (they pass on an empty art set), then terrain, player, creatures, items, structures, crossing; each group lands with its tests and leaves the game runnable.
- `DevIslandTerrain` keeps `build` and `fill`; its `draw` and `COLORS` go once every caller uses the tile builder. `player_placeholder.png` goes when the player sheet lands.
- Rollback is a revert: no save data, settings or network messages change, apart from the extra `look` key in the player spawn payload, which an older peer ignores.

**D25. Layered look, ready for `player-looks` (owner decision, 2026-11-08).** The owner chose how a balsero gets their look: the background sets the outfit and one signature item drawn on the body (the fisherman's hat, the músico's guitar on the back), the player picks man or woman, skin tone, hair and clothing colour in the lobby (a title-screen screen in solo), and extra hairs, hats and outfits unlock when found in a run, remembered per install. This change does not build the picker or the unlocks; it draws the player from layers (body row, outfit, hair, item) on MetroCity's 32×32 grid through a `PlayerLookData` of part ids, so `player-looks` only adds choices. Cost: every hand-made pose (D3) is drawn once per layer part it can show with. This pass ships the body rows, one default outfit (a T-shirt and shorts) and the eight MetroCity hairs, so its pose frames cover those; parts added later must bring their own pose frames, and the frame-budget check counts each part. MetroCity has no beards, moustaches or hats other than the police cap: those are hand-made hair or item parts in `player-looks`.
