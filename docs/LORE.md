# 90 Millas — Lore

The historical backdrop for writers, with a source for every claim. The run is set in the present day. 1994 and the older waves live in found texts, wrecks and the memories of older characters (Ramón, Tomás, Alfredo), never in the run's date. The canonical present year is still open (`TASKS.md` §11).

Rules for using it:

- **No real person is named or drawn**, and nobody real is quoted. Several sources name private families, officers and judges. None of those names may enter the game.
- **General history may appear in game text** (found texts, item descriptions, loading tips, NPC memories): sections 1–3, 4 except the "Handle with care" bullet, 5 (first two bullets and the chugs bullet), 6 (first three bullets), 7 (second bullet) and 8 except the gull video.
- **Specific incidents are writer inspiration only** and are never retold with dates, places or numbers: the 1994 tugboat, the 2003 and 2004 car-boats, the 2014 and 2022 windsurfers, the living-room raft, the 2006 piling, the 2016 lighthouse, the 2016 and 2025 strandings and the 2024 gull video.
- **No brands, logos or real songs.** Stations are generic ("la emisora oficial", "una emisora de Miami").
- **Nothing jokes about the dead or the missing.** The humor stays with the living, the gear and the system. The game never shows a death count.
- Religious objects are flavor only and carry no mechanic.
- Sources are summarized, never quoted. Where a link has died or a claim stands on one source, it says so.

## 1. Ninety miles, more or less

- The marker at the southernmost point of Key West says 90 miles to Cuba. The nearest Cuban point is about 94 statute miles away, and Havana is farther. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southernmost_Point_Buoy
- The Florida Current, the start of the Gulf Stream, runs north between the two coasts. Sailing guides put its core at roughly 2 to 4.5 knots. https://www.amnautical.com/blogs/the-mariners-blog/tips-for-crossing-the-gulf-stream · https://glossary.ametsoc.org/wiki/florida-current/
- It can carry a raft past Florida altogether. In September 1994 fishing captains off Hatteras reported Cuban rafts in the stream, and at least one homemade boat drifted to the Outer Banks. In 2004 a Navy helicopter found one rafter about 50 miles off North Carolina. Open item: the 1994 article returns 410 Gone, so an archived copy still has to be found and cited. https://scholar.lib.vt.edu/VA-news/VA-Pilot/issues/1994/vp940914/09140474.htm · https://www.latinamericanstudies.org/immigration/drifting.htm (the 2004 rescue only)

## 2. Three generations of leaving

- **Camarioca, 1965.** In autumn 1965 the government let exiles come by sea to the small port of Camarioca, beside Varadero, to fetch relatives. In December the boats gave way to twice-daily flights from Varadero to Miami, which ran until April 1973 and carried about 260,000 people. Sources disagree on the number of boat departures (about 3,000 to about 5,000). https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1964-68v32/d308 · https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/applied-and-social-sciences-magazines/camarioca-boat-lift · https://media.defense.gov/2020/Jul/02/2002356759/0/0/0/CAMARIOCA1965.PDF
- **Mariel, 1980.** On 1 April 1980 a bus was driven through the gate of the Peruvian embassy in Havana. Peru would not hand the group over, the government withdrew the guards, and within two days more than 10,000 people had crowded into the grounds. The government then opened the port of Mariel to anyone who could arrange a boat, and exiles hired fishing and shrimp boats. About 125,000 Cubans arrived before the boatlift ended in October. https://text-message.blogs.archives.gov/2021/08/03/the-causes-and-effects-of-the-mariel-boatlift · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariel_boatlift
- **Actos de repudio.** During Mariel, crowds organized through the neighborhood committees gathered outside the homes of people who had asked to leave, shouting and throwing eggs. Some turned violent. https://havanatimes.org/?p=26118 · https://text-message.blogs.archives.gov/2021/08/03/the-causes-and-effects-of-the-mariel-boatlift
- **Gusano, mariposa, escoria.** *Gusano* (worm) was the official insult for those who left. After the 1978 dialogue, exiles began visiting family in 1979, arriving with gifts and money, and Cubans joked that the gusanos had become *mariposas* (butterflies). After Mariel the usual insult became *escoria* (scum). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gusano · https://oncubanews.com/cuba/nacion-y-emigracion/enfrentar-el-retorno-las-visitas-de-la-comunidad-de-1979

## 3. The Special Period

- After the Soviet Union fell, Cuba's oil imports dropped from about 13 million tonnes in 1989 to about 5 million in 1993. The economy shrank by about a third, and blackouts (*apagones*) could last 16 hours. Estimated daily calories fell from about 3,050 to about 2,100. https://www.ipsnews.net/?p=73911 · https://new.scielo.br/j/ea/a/ZDsfdYJymh7TwVGB7P6wwDH/?lang=en · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Period
- Since the early 1960s households have bought a monthly quota of basics with the *libreta* (ration book), and lines (*colas*) form when deliveries arrive. In the 1990s the quota shrank. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationing_in_Cuba
- With no fuel, Cuba imported more than a million Chinese bicycles in the early 1990s (estimates run from 1.2 to 1.5 million) and sold them cheaply. No spare parts were imported, so people made their own inner tubes and chain oil. https://cubanstudies.history.ufl.edu/special-period-in-a-time-of-peace/bicicleta-China-chinese-bicycle-june-1995 · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Period · https://www.ibike.org/economics/cuba-bike-policy.htm
- Scarcity gave the era its dishes. *Bistec de toronja* was grapefruit pith, breaded and fried, remembered in memoirs and even printed in a Special Period how-to book from the armed forces' own publisher. *Milordo* was sugar water served to guests in place of coffee or juice. https://havanatimes.org/?p=68989 · https://www.wlrn.org/post/technological-disobedience-how-cubans-manipulate-everyday-technologies-survival · https://en.cocina-cubana.com/articulos/cuban-special-period-meals · https://oncubanews.com/cuba/heraldica-del-periodo-especial (milordo only)

## 4. August 1994

- After months of boat hijackings in Havana Bay, a rumor that boats were coming from Miami drew thousands to the Malecón on 5 August 1994: the first large protest since 1959, called the *Maleconazo*. About a week later the government said it would stop preventing departures by sea. More than 35,000 rafters left that year. https://scholar.library.miami.edu/digital/exhibits/show/guantanamo/crisis · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maleconazo · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Cuban_rafter_crisis
- Cojímar, a fishing town east of Havana, was a main departure beach. People traded houses and cars for boats or homemade catamarans. Press photos show inner-tube rafts and a finished raft put up for sale by a builder who had changed his mind. https://havanatimes.org/?p=1652 · https://www.latinamericanstudies.org/balsero-rafter-crisis-1.htm · https://time.com/archive/6725910/the-view-from-cojimar/
- On 19 August 1994 the United States said Cubans picked up at sea would be taken to the Guantánamo naval base, not to Florida. The Coast Guard operation picked up about 30,000. https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/ops/able-vigil.htm · https://scholar.library.miami.edu/digital/exhibits/show/guantanamo/crisis
- In the base's tent camps the rafters made art from tent canvas, plywood, house paint, pen ink and melted ration packets, taught each other classes, built improvised gyms and printed their own newspapers. In May 1995 the U.S. announced that most would be admitted. https://scholar.library.miami.edu/digital/exhibits/show/guantanamo/creation · https://scholar.library.miami.edu/digital/exhibits/show/guantanamo/detention · https://gitmomemory.org/p11/?r=4329 · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Cuban_rafter_crisis
- **Handle with care.** On 13 July 1994 an old state tugboat carrying 72 people out of Havana was hosed and rammed by pursuing state boats until it sank. The Inter-American Commission on Human Rights recorded 41 dead, ten of them minors. This is background for writers only: never a set piece, a mechanic, a found text or a joke, and the AI prompt bans the topic. https://cidh.oas.org/annualrep/96eng/cuba11436.htm · https://www.amnesty.org/en/documents/amr25/013/1997/en/

## 5. What people built

- Most last-resort rafts of the early 1990s sat on truck inner tubes, sometimes with barrels, styrofoam, plywood or house doors. Builders wrapped the tubes in canvas or plastic against shark bites and sun. Between October 1991 and June 1994 the average time at sea was almost four days. https://dukespace.lib.duke.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/fc78cdac-0dd2-4dd8-836e-ed701a708f50/content · https://projects.journalism.berkeley.edu/cubans2001/story-balseros.html · https://time.com/archive/6725910/the-view-from-cojimar/
- After taking people off a raft, the Coast Guard usually tried to sink or destroy it as a hazard to navigation. A craft left afloat was logged and spray-painted (for example 'OK') to show it had been checked. In 1994 Havana residents assumed that raft wreckage washing onto their beaches came from people who had not survived. https://projects.journalism.berkeley.edu/cubans2001/story-balseros.html · https://www.themoscowtimes.com/archive/cuban-refugees-face-waves-piracy-and-sharks · https://wptv.com/news/region-c-palm-beach-county/riviera-beach/makeshift-raft-washes-ashore-on-singer-island-police-say
- A Havana family in 1994 built a raft in their living room that would not fit through the front door. This is a secondhand account in an opinion column: inspiration only. https://oncubanews.com/en/opinion/columns/in-plain-words/migratory-things-iv/
- **The floating truck and car (inspiration only).** In 2003 a group turned a 1950s pickup into a boat: empty 55-gallon drums as pontoons and a propeller driven off the drive shaft. Everyone aboard was sent back and the truck was sunk with gunfire so it would not inspire copycats. In 2004 some of the same people tried a sealed 1950s sedan with a welded steel bow and no drums; it was stopped off the Keys. https://latinamericanstudies.org/immigration/truck-boat-copycats.htm · https://www.semissourian.com/news/cuban-migrants-convert-1951-pickup-truck-to-raft-115092 · https://latinamericanstudies.org/immigration/sunk.htm · https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp/wbna4163593
- **Chugs.** Keys officials and Coast Guard crews call homemade Cuban motorboats *chugs*, after the sound of their engines: small air-cooled or diesel engines, sometimes a converted car engine. Later chugs have aluminum, copper-sheet or fiberglass hulls of about 20 to 25 feet; cruder 1990s boats used tarps and expanding foam. https://www.npca.org/articles/1861-the-meaning-of-the-chug · https://keysweekly.com/42/freedom-floats-cubans-risking-death-to-cross-the-straits-on-chugs
- **Windsurf boards (inspiration only).** In February 2014 three men left Jibacoa on separate boards and lost sight of each other about four hours out. One landed on a Key West beach about nine and a half hours after leaving, a second was found drifting south of the Keys two days later, and the third was found four days out, too weak to move, on the uninhabited Marquesas Keys. In 2022 two more Cubans reached the Keys on boards. https://www.cbsnews.com/miami/news/cuban-migrant-windsurfs-to-fl-keys · https://www.yahoo.com/news/cuban-windsurfer-rescued-four-days-sea-u-coast-162145041.html?ref=gs · https://havanatimes.org/news/cuban-reaches-us-by-windsurfing · https://www.local10.com/news/local/2022/10/12/2-cuban-migrants-detained-after-arriving-in-keys-on-windsurfing-boards-official-says

## 6. The rules of the water

- In the September 1994 accord the U.S. promised at least 20,000 legal entries a year. In the May 1995 accord it agreed that Cubans intercepted at sea would be taken back to Cuba. https://clintonwhitehouse6.archives.gov/1994/09/1994-09-09-press-secretary-statement-on-agreement-with-cuba.html · https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/joint-statement-with-the-republic-cuba-normalization-migration
- Part of that quota was a visa lottery Cubans called *el bombo*. About 189,000 people entered the first draw and about 435,000 the second, in 1996. https://migration.ucdavis.edu/mn/more.php?id=1574 · https://www.spokesman.com/stories/1996/mar/13/cubans-play-lottery-for-us-visas/
- The result was "wet foot, dry foot": caught on the water, sent back; on dry land, allowed to stay. The policy ended on 12 January 2017. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_feet,_dry_feet_policy · https://fox61.com/2017/01/12/us-ending-wet-foot-dry-foot-policy-for-cubans/ · https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2017/01/14/us-policy-change-leaves-cuban-migrants-stranded/
- Officials and courts argued about what counts as land (inspiration only). In 2006 fifteen Cubans who reached a disconnected piling of an old Keys bridge were sent back as "wet foot", and a judge later ruled the removal wrong. In 2016 a court ruled that an offshore reef lighthouse was not dry land. https://www.sunjournal.com/2006/01/10/15-cubans-reach-abandoned-bridge-florida-sent-home-dispute-dry-foot-policy/ · https://www.jurist.org/news/2006/03/federal-judge-says-deported-cubans/?edition=US · https://www.foxnews.com/world/judge-rules-against-cuban-migrants-who-clung-to-florida-keys-lighthouse

## 7. Stranded, dead, missing

- Many rafts end up on uninhabited Bahamian cays instead of Florida. In 2016 a group survived about nine days on a Cay Sal Bank cay until a patrol plane spotted them and dropped food and water; in 2025 aircrews dropped food, water and a radio to another stranded group before it was picked up (inspiration only). http://www.tribune242.com/news/2016/jun/02/cuban-migrants-rescued-after-being-stranded-nine-d · https://kmph.com/news/nation-world/37-cuban-migrants-rescued-by-coast-guard-after-being-stranded-on-uninhabited-islands-cay-sal-bank-anguilla-cay-us-coast-guard-news-january-13-2025
- Nobody knows how many rafters died. Published estimates vary enormously and there is no official count. The game never shows a death count. https://www.themoscowtimes.com/archive/cuban-refugees-face-waves-piracy-and-sharks · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balseros

## 8. Birds and music

- The laughing gull, the one gull that breeds in Florida, is called *galleguito* in Cuba, and it begs and steals food from beachgoers. Cuban name: https://www.birdscaribbean.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/List-of-hunted-trapped-birds-Graphic-Caption-compressed.pdf · Florida breeding (of the nine gull species regular in Florida, only this one breeds there): https://nsis.org/bird/sp/wb-gull.html · handouts among its foods: https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Laughing_Gull/food
- An August 2024 aggregator report described an unverified TikTok video in which a gull sat on a rafter's head and the rafters took it as a sign that land was near. No second outlet corroborates it, and the video's date, place and authenticity are unknown. The gull finale (`docs/BESTIARY.md`, "The final passenger") is invention inspired by that anecdote and the gull's real habits, not documented history, and reuses none of the video's people or words. https://www.cubaheadlines.com/articles/287181 (no corroborating outlet found)
- Migrating songbirds crossing the Gulf of Mexico are known to land, exhausted, on boats and offshore structures. The cited study reports this secondhand, in its introduction and references; a direct observational study would be a stronger citation. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4655507
- The frigatebird (*rabihorcado*) almost never lands on water and steals food from other seabirds in the air. https://keysweekly.com/42/wild-things-piracy-on-the-low-seas/ · https://allaboutbirds.org/guide/Magnificent_Frigatebird/overview
- Brown boobies (*pájaro bobo*) often rest on buoys and channel markers, and now and then one rides a boat. https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Brown_Booby/lifehistory · https://www.cbsnews.com/boston/news/boaters-get-glimpse-of-rare-booby-bird-in-boston-harbor/ (one example)
- Early Cuban *son* groups played bass by blowing into a *botija*, an earthenware jar of the kind once used to ship olive oil and wine from Spain. The game's botija is a fuel jug, a fictional stand-in. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botija_(instrument)
